# 1. CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY — EVOLUTION, NOT REPLACEMENT

My current application already has a design theme and visual identity that I want to retain.

**Your objective is NOT to replace my current design with an unrelated visual style. Your objective is to transform the existing application into the best possible version of itself.**

The current interface is the starting point and primary visual reference.

Preserve its recognisable visual identity, overall aesthetic direction, brand personality and strongest existing design elements.

However, you have extensive creative freedom to improve, extend, modernise, restructure and elevate that foundation.

### You are free to:

- Completely redesign weak components.
- Improve typography, spacing, alignment and visual hierarchy.
- Introduce more sophisticated page layouts.
- Refine and expand the existing colour palette.
- Introduce complementary visual elements.
- Improve navigation and information architecture.
- Add sophisticated but purposeful animations and micro-interactions.
- Redesign cards, forms, buttons, tables and dashboards.
- Introduce new components and interaction patterns.
- Reorganise pages to improve user experience.
- Reimagine individual screens where necessary.
- Enhance the application's visual identity without replacing it.
- Challenge poor existing design decisions.
- Remove unnecessary visual clutter.
- Introduce entirely new design concepts where the existing approach fundamentally limits quality.

### You must NOT:

- Randomly replace the application's theme.
- Force an unrelated aesthetic onto the product.
- Copy another application's design.
- Change elements simply for the sake of change.
- Sacrifice usability for visual effects.
- Break existing functionality to accommodate a new design.
- Introduce generic AI-generated dashboard aesthetics.
- Create inconsistent visual styles between pages.
- Overuse gradients, glassmorphism, animations or decorative elements.

### The intended result

When an existing user sees the finished product, their reaction should be:

"This is clearly the same application, but it looks and feels dramatically more polished, sophisticated, consistent and professionally designed."

**Preserve the soul of the existing design. Perfect its execution. Extend it wherever necessary.**

# 4. PHASE THREE — UNDERSTAND THE EXISTING DESIGN DNA

This is one of the most important phases.

Create a Design DNA Report covering:

Identify which elements should remain stable and which can be improved.

Separate the product's genuine identity from inconsistencies or accidental design decisions.

## Three levels of change

Classify proposed changes into three categories.

### REFINE

Keep the existing structure but elevate the execution.

Examples:

- Better typography
- More consistent spacing
- Cleaner alignment
- Improved colours
- Refined shadows and borders
- Better interactive states

### EVOLVE

Preserve the existing visual identity while making meaningful structural improvements.

Examples:

- Redesigned dashboard layout
- Improved navigation
- New component architecture
- More sophisticated information hierarchy
- Enhanced interactions

### REIMAGINE

Substantially redesign a screen or component that is fundamentally limiting the application.

Examples:

- Rebuilding a confusing onboarding experience
- Replacing an ineffective pricing layout
- Reorganising a poorly structured workflow

Even when reimagining a screen, its design must still feel consistent with the overall application.

**Do not force every page into a complete redesign. Apply the appropriate level of intervention.**

Make the design feel intentionally crafted for desktop, tablet and mobile rather than merely shrinking desktop screens.

### Brand identity

Maintain and strengthen the original application's visual personality.

The finished product should feel distinctive and memorable.

## Agreed Weekly Starter subscription — required scope

Include the previously agreed Weekly Starter subscription alongside Monthly and Yearly in the research, Figma explorations, final paywall, implementation plan and QA. Weekly is a required product option, not an obsolete concept or a trip-only offer.

### Agreed weekly offer

- Plan: Starter Weekly.
- Target US price: US$5.49, charged every seven days.
- Allowance: 45 live AI tutor minutes per seven-day subscription cycle.
- Payment: Weekly and Monthly are charged immediately. The seven-day free trial is Yearly-only and requires verified eligibility. The proposed 45-minute yearly trial cap still needs approval; it is separate from the agreed 45-minute Weekly allowance.
- Renewal: automatically every seven days until cancelled.
- Unused minutes do not roll over. Cancellation stops future renewal; remaining paid access follows the verified entitlement and expiry.
- Keep the reassurance copy exactly: “No Commitment, Cancel Anytime”. Show the billed amount, seven-day recurrence and automatic renewal clearly beside the purchase action.
- Weekly serves hesitant learners and shorter-term needs as well as trip preparation. Describe it as a smaller initial payment, never as cheaper overall than Monthly or Yearly.

US$5.49 is the founder-selected US target from the paywall research report. Do not convert it into an invented UK price, mix currencies in one comparison, or present a design fixture as a configured live product. Use verified localized store prices for production. Keep other plan prices, allowances and trial eligibility tied to their approved catalogue and store data.

### Figma and UX coverage

- Show Weekly as a genuine, selectable billing option in the main Starter paywall, with clear selected and unselected states.
- Design the Weekly, Monthly and Yearly comparison so the essential billed amounts, allowance, renewal terms and primary action fit the normal phone viewport. Allow accessible reflow and scrolling at larger text sizes.
- Preserve the selected duration when returning from plan details or an interrupted purchase.
- Include Weekly purchase opening, cancellation, failure, pending verification, verified success, restore, active subscription, cancelled-but-active, expired access, billing recovery and plan-change states where supported by the actual product.
- Keep Max discoverable separately; do not invent a Max Weekly product.
- A Weekly + Yearly comparison may be explored as an experiment, but retain the agreed three-option baseline and do not claim a conversion improvement without measured results.

### Implementation prerequisites and acceptance criteria

The current native catalogue has monthly and yearly products, so treat weekly billing support as explicit implementation work rather than a copy-only change. Document the store product, purchase provider mapping, catalogue contract, entitlement verification, seven-day allowance window, renewal handling, restore and subscription management dependencies.

A Weekly renewal must grant the approved 45-minute cycle allowance, never inherit a monthly 180- or 300-minute allowance. Verify cycle boundaries, duplicate or delayed renewal events, cancellation, expiry, restoration and switching between durations in safe test environments. Do not change live billing configuration or run real charges as part of design verification.

Include Weekly in the screen inventory, paywall strategy, implementation plan, coverage report and QA report. Clearly distinguish approved design, implemented code and configured/live availability. Preserve the approval checkpoint before a broad frontend implementation.
