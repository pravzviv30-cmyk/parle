# Luvani UX production review

Expanded review: five videos, interface copy, playful motion and colour | 4 October 2026

**Verdict: keep the visual identity, simplify the journey, and hold production release until the trust and device gates pass.** Luvani looks coherent and has several strong learning features. Its weakest point is the distance between seeing a useful French example and producing French that becomes something the learner can use again.

The first run spends six screens demonstrating, asking and explaining before account creation. The plan gives a giant daily time target more prominence than the first useful task. Several later screens repeat the same microphone and live-minute explanation. On a small phone, the first scene can show its start button before its title or example line is visible.

**The most serious finding is functional.** The scripted lesson can fall back to a demo driver without a production-only refusal. In voice mode, tapping its microphone supplies the reference answer and fabricated sound observations. Its completion handler uses the normal session, activity and review-writing path. The local preview reproduces the demo entry; production reachability is established by source inspection, rather than by a deployed production test.

The video offers useful ideas about defaults, progress, value and ownership. Its fake-progress, threatened-deletion and misleading price-framing examples should not become Luvani features. A production language app earns return visits through reliable help, visible learning and usable saved work.

**Expansion verdict:** cut repeated interface prose, make the first useful action the strongest colour moment, and animate selection, durable saves and genuinely earned outcomes. The existing blue, coral, sun and mint palette can feel much more alive through better placement. Continuous decorative motion, extra setup questions and a borrowed mascot would add distraction before the app earns a return visit.

- **First:** remove production access to simulated learner evidence and complete the existing release acceptance work.
- **Next:** deliver a small, genuine first attempt; compress the plan and scene briefing; make one relevant action lead across tabs.
- **Then:** surface the actual phrase in the first recap view; simplify optional social entry; apply the copy, colour and motion specifications in this expanded report and validate them with beginners.
- This is an audit and change specification. Application code and product settings were not redesigned as part of this review.

## What the first three video principles say

**Production interpretation:** reduce the work required to reach a worthwhile learning moment. Do not optimize only the percentage that reaches the account or payment screen. A learner who can finish a task and knows what has been saved is more valuable than a learner pushed through unexplained choices.

### Smart defaults at 00:20 to 01:44

The booking example replaces blank fields with useful initial selections. The point is to lower avoidable choice and effort. The video invokes the jam assortment study and claims most people keep defaults. For Luvani, suggest a beginner starting route, a low-pressure routine and an available next task. Keep changes easy. Do not invent a personal goal, prefill unknown credentials, enable data sharing or select a costly purchase merely because the video says every field should have a default.

### Goal gradient at 01:44 to 03:11

The loyalty-card example makes a task appear already underway and shows progress toward completion. The video recommends counting work already done and even creating an artificial head start. For Luvani, show actual setup completed and real task steps remaining. Keep setup, assisted practice and independent ability separate. Listening to a sample or making an account must not become fabricated French mastery.

### Reciprocity at 03:14 to 05:06

The SEO example shows useful results before asking for signup, instead of blurring everything behind a form. The useful transfer is experiencing value before commitment. Luvani already gives recorded French and an optional coaching demonstration before signup. The next improvement is a genuine small learner action with understandable feedback. The video's claim that a gift creates a debt is not a reason to guilt a learner into signup or payment.

## What the remaining video principles say

**06:29 to 07:45 is a course promotion.** Its code, guarantee and sales pitch are not product requirements. The ending at 10:28 to 10:57 summarizes the six principles, including artificial progress. Nothing in the attachment authorizes following its calls to buy, like, follow or use promotional tools.

The video does not establish typography, color, accessibility, French pedagogy, microphone reliability or clinical habit formation standards. Recommendations on those topics below come from the app review and production requirements, with their evidence identified separately.

### IKEA and endowment effects at 05:06 to 06:29

The video moves between the value of making something and the value of feeling ownership. It proposes naming and customizing a product before signup. These are related but distinct claims. For Luvani, the strongest personal artifact is French the learner actually produced, with its useful correction and context. Tutor choice can support comfort; additional profile questions are not automatically meaningful ownership.

### Loss aversion at 07:45 to 09:02

The example threatens deletion of files and uses a shaming dismissal to create urgency. Luvani should reject that treatment. A truthful allowance notice can explain that connected tutoring pauses when live minutes run out while saved phrases and free practice remain. The learner must not fear losing their learning because they take a day off or cancel.

### Contrast effect at 09:02 to 10:28

The video makes a recurring subscription seem small beside a costly laptop. For Luvani, present real plans in comparable units: full store price, billing period, monthly minutes and realistic usage examples. Comparing a monthly fee with a one-time product price conceals the annual commitment. Do not copy the 2.6 percent framing or invent a higher anchor.

## How much to trust the research claims

### Choice overload is a context finding

Columbia's Choice Architecture Lab describes the 24 versus 6 jam experiment and approximately 3 versus 30 percent purchase rates. That supports the historical example. It does not predict a tenfold Luvani conversion increase or prove that fewer choices always help. The video's 70 to 90 percent default-retention claim has no identifiable supporting source in the supplied material. [Columbia Choice Architecture Lab](https://cal.gsb.columbia.edu/content/about-us).

### The car wash attribution needs correction

Nunes and Dreze's endowed-progress paper uses eight required purchases versus ten with two already credited, with 19 versus 34 percent redemption in its car wash study. The video associates this example with Columbia. Columbia's separate Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng paper includes a coffee loyalty study with ten versus twelve spaces and two bonus stamps. The ideas are related, but the specific experiments should not be merged. [Nunes and Dreze paper uploaded by its author](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23547282_The_Endowed_Progress_Effect_How_Artificial_Advancement_Increases_Effort); [Columbia goal gradient paper](https://www.columbia.edu/~rk566/Session4/Goal-Gradient_Illusionary_Goal_Progress.pdf).

### Making something must end in a useful success

Norton, Mochon and Ariely's working paper finds increased valuation when people successfully complete what they build; the effect dissipates when they fail to complete it or destroy it. This supports helping a learner create a useful phrase, rather than adding setup labor for its own sake. Transfer to a language app remains a hypothesis. [Harvard Business School working paper](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/11-091.pdf).

### Treat sales multipliers as unsupported here

The free-sample uplift of up to 2,000 percent, universal two-times loss framing and claim that threats win every time are not verified Luvani results. No conversion, retention or willingness-to-pay forecast in this report is based on those figures. The report does not attempt a comprehensive literature review of all six effects.

## Remove simulated learner evidence from production

**Evidence:** PracticeTab.tsx:201-210; tutor/gate.ts:166-179; RootNavigator.tsx:183-215 and 258-272; SessionScreen.tsx:133-140 and 169-175. Screenshot O9. Relationship to the video: experienced value and ownership must be real, not manufactured progress.

### F01 P0 Source confirmed and locally reproduced

Practice's recommended sound lesson opens even when its health probe fails. RootNavigator mounts SessionScreen whenever live && user is false; this fallback has no development-only condition. In voice mode, SessionScreen's microphone callback passes prompt.turn.target to speak(), which adds mockObservations. Its header discloses Offline demo and no microphone recording, but the route retains normal lesson completion behavior.

### Why this is a release blocker

The same finish callback records the summary, activity and lesson review material. Source therefore permits a simulated voice answer and invented sound evidence to enter the learner's normal history. Labeling the screen a demo does not make that history trustworthy. Typed input is actual typed text and avoids mock phonemes; the risk must be described specifically rather than accusing every offline interaction of fabrication.

### Required change

Remove this driver from production navigation. Offer genuine local listening, recording comparison or authored typed practice instead. Any development demonstration must be explicitly isolated, must not claim the learner spoke, and must not write simulated records into normal learning, ability or pronunciation evidence. Decide deliberately which legitimate local activities count as practice, independently of live billing.

### Acceptance

With a real authenticated account, a production build and live=false, every lesson entry refuses the simulated driver or reaches a genuine local task. A microphone tap without recording cannot manufacture a transcript, sound diagnosis, earned ability or review record. A development demo leaves normal history unchanged. Confirm legacy demo records can be identified before proposing any cleanup; do not blindly delete learner records.

## Make choices and progress tell the truth

## Make the next step relevant and available

### F09 P1 One coherent next task

**Evidence:** O, S. Home's date-based weather phrase, Practice's bonjour lesson and Speak's connection retry compete as first actions. Tier/date selection does not encode the current cafe task.

### User impact and decision

H. The app asks the beginner to choose between learning methods and contexts before they have a stable routine. **Change:** Use a shared recommendation policy: resume unfinished meaningful work, practise relevant owned material when due, then attempt the next authored scene. With no owned material and no live tutor, offer a cafe-related genuine local task. Keep the daily collection as exploration rather than the fallback that defines the core journey.

## Proposed first visit and return journey

**Design proposal, not an implemented or tested redesign.** Use the current palette, font and authored cafe content. The structural change is making useful work appear before customization and letting the learner return directly to it.

**First visit:** hear or read the cafe exchange; complete one small local authored response; see a useful line and honest guidance; accept a suggested beginner route; create an account with necessary age and data choices; begin the short real scene; leave with one actual phrase and a clear saved state. Do not promise pre-account spoken assessment or a tailored curriculum that does not exist.

**Return visit:** resume unfinished work or practise a relevant kept phrase, then attempt the next scene. If live tutoring is unavailable, provide the same context as genuine local practice. The daily collection and optional social challenge remain secondary exploration.

**Scene briefing structure:** task and outcome first, approximate duration second, a single optional example, and Start speaking / Type instead. A short notice says that connected tutoring uses live minutes. Detailed processing and controls remain accessible from named help links.

**Recap structure:** You said plus the real line; Try this plus a useful correction only when supported; Kept for you with save status; optional Practise this phrase; Finish. Additional sound/ability evidence appears only when its provenance is reliable.

## Apply the craft video to Luvani's actual product

**Qualification:** the creator's anecdotes, cost examples and claims about what users feel do not establish a Luvani effect size. ## Fifth video: qualify the psychology before using it

**Research scope:** the labor paper's abstract and relevant discussion were inspected; the peak-end, choice and isolation research claims on this page use their public research abstracts. This is a targeted verification, not a full systematic literature review. Application to Luvani remains a design hypothesis to test.

### Goal gradient does not justify invented learning

The report's existing Columbia goal-gradient and endowed-progress references concern specific experimental tasks. They do not establish that a six-screen setup rail teaches French, that streak pressure improves learning, or that an onboarding estimate is accurate. Use completed actions and known remaining steps. The current screen is a position indicator, not necessarily a completed action.

### Peak-end includes the ending

The established name is peak-end rule; auto-captions render the video's phrase as peak end goal. The account of only remembering highs and lows is too absolute and omits the ending. Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier's 1993 cold-water experiment found retrospective preference could favor a longer painful episode with a less painful ending. The [publisher abstract](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x/) supports a bounded memory/evaluation finding, not universal amnesia for the middle or an app retention multiplier.

### Provider effort is different from the learner's effort

Buell and Norton's [2011 labor-illusion paper](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Norton_Michael_The%20labor%20illusion%20How%20operational_f4269b70-3732-4fc4-8113-72d0c47533e0.pdf) studies operational transparency in five simulated online travel/dating experiments. Perceived provider effort and reciprocity help explain valuation; the effect depends on outcomes, and unfavorable results can make transparent waiting worse. This differs from the user's own construction effort in the IKEA effect. It does not validate extra language-practice friction or an obligatory delay. Luvani's recommendation is truthful disclosure during real work, with results delivered when ready.

### Distinctiveness is relative, not simply brighter

Hunt and Lamb's [2001 isolation experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11713872/) examine memory for items that differ within a surrounding context. They treat similarity and difference together. This supports testing deliberate emphasis; it does not prove that a louder colour, larger mascot or extra animation improves French recall. A dominant daily-minute number can be distinctive yet emphasize the wrong thing.

### Fewer choices is not a universal law

Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd's [2010 meta-analysis abstract](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235) reports 63 conditions from 50 experiments, N = 5,036, with an average effect near zero and substantial variation. Some studies favor fewer options; others do not. Use the observed competing recommendations and novice task confusion to justify a Luvani prototype, rather than treating five tabs or a long list as automatically defective.

**Trigger:** After a chosen starting level changes the genuine starting scene. **Proposed motion:** 220-320 ms: new scene/context fades into a stable summary area. **Fallback and truth:** Goal context may be shown but must not imply a different route. No animated layout jump; old/new text remain accessible. Final summary immediately with reduced motion.

### M03 First phrase kept

**Trigger:** After the exact local save operation succeeds. **Proposed motion:** 380-520 ms: a small card/kept icon settles, with an optional single success haptic. **Fallback and truth:** Render phrase and Saved on this phone before motion ends. Sync pending has separate wording. Failed save uses retry, no success animation.

### M04 Earned scene completion

**Trigger:** After reliable task-achievement criteria confirm an eligible genuine result. **Proposed motion:** 500-700 ms: reuse and refine OnceConfetti, contained away from French text; one event. **Fallback and truth:** First actual quote/correction remains visible and Finish stays usable. Reduced motion uses a static earned mark. No success for fabricated/demo speech or unsupported ability.

## Motion specification: practice, help and live state

F24. Animate only a confirmed state change. Motion is interruptible, does not delay an action, and never substitutes for a caption or status. These are starting ranges for device prototyping; repeat use and accessibility determine the final choice.

### M05 Local practice completion

**Trigger:** After a real local sitting is saved with accurate assistance scope. **Proposed motion:** 260-380 ms: small check settle and phrase count, if useful. **Fallback and truth:** A practice finish is not necessarily earned scene ability. Static Practised / recalled scope remains, with truthful save state.

### M06 Help revealed

**Trigger:** After explicit Show the phrase / help. **Proposed motion:** 180-240 ms: contained fade; no distracting movement of the whole task. **Fallback and truth:** Keep assistance easy. Mark helped evidence correctly; no reward removed or shame. Reduced motion reveals immediately with a proper announcement.

### M07 Return to a kept phrase

**Trigger:** When deliberately opening a specific kept line from its source context. **Proposed motion:** 220-320 ms: short transition preserving title/phrase context. **Fallback and truth:** Avoid a long flying-card transition or shrinking text. Missing source/read failure gives retry, not a decorative blank library.

### M08 Voice state

**Trigger:** Only while actual capture, actual tutor audio or an actual request state exists. **Proposed motion:** Keep the existing controlled orb behavior and explicit state label. **Fallback and truth:** Stop on exit, interruption, timeout and background as required. No waveform implying recording when mic is closed; reduced motion retains caption and state glyph.

## Motion implementation: start small, test the runtime

**Before implementation:** apps/mobile/AGENTS.md requires the exact [Expo 57 documentation](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/). Update design contracts and tests deliberately, then run the repository gate and native builds. This report installed no animation packages and changed no app code.

### Recommended first implementation

Reuse React Native Animated and the existing shared motion and haptic helpers for press, opacity and transform transitions. No new animation dependency is needed for the first selection/save prototypes. Preserve the one shared press spring. New motion should be named, centrally timed, cancelable and linked to actual domain events rather than component mounting.

### Rive is a later option for a real stateful character

The installed app uses Expo 57.0.18 and React Native 0.86.3; Rive, Lottie and Reanimated are not currently app dependencies. Rive's current React Native runtime uses Nitro and its Expo guide requires a development build because custom native code is not supported in Expo Go. Meeting published minimum versions does not prove compatibility with this exact app. A separate iOS/Android build and performance spike is required before adopting it. [Rive React Native requirements](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/react-native/react-native); [Rive with Expo](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/react-native/adding-rive-to-expo).

### Lottie is a later option for authored one-shot illustrations

Evaluate a licensed, compact vector asset only after there is a specific earned or explanatory event that needs it. Preview the actual runtime on both platforms, including font/colour differences, masks, asset decoding and memory. A vendor's compression result is not Luvani's performance budget. Ship a static fallback and keep phrases/prices as real UI text, rather than baking them into an animation.

### Keep the native performance boundary

Transform and opacity are the first choice; do not drive per-frame layout or SVG geometry through the existing native-driver helpers. Stop timers and animations on exit, account change and app background, without changing audio cleanup or request limits. Never keep an invisible loop alive behind another screen. [React Native animations](https://reactnative.dev/docs/animations).

## Motion acceptance and treatments to remove

**Remove from the proposed design:** looping scene art, bouncing tab icons, confetti on every tap, animated countdown pressure, paywall celebration before a purchase result, artificial plan generation, parallax reading surfaces and mascot guilt. A calm static error with a usable action serves the learner better than treating a failure as a cheerful reward.

[Apple motion guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion) favors brief, precise feedback, optional motion and cancellation. [WCAG animation from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html) is Level AAA guidance for disabling nonessential interaction animation; this report does not mislabel it as an AA certification result.

| Case | Pass condition | Reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reduce Motion | Cold-start uncertainty is safe; toggling the OS preference mid-animation stops motion and shows the same final state. | Content or a result withheld until animation completes. |
| Rapid input / navigation | Repeated tap, back, exit and reentry cannot duplicate saves, haptics or earned celebration. | Replay triggered by remount or stale async response. |
| Assistive technology | Status announced once; focus follows the task; phrase text remains readable; meaning survives without colour, motion or sound. | A bouncing icon or confetti as the only success signal. |
| Failure / pending | Actual Saving, Saved locally, sync pending and failed states stay distinct. Purchase success waits for authoritative confirmation. | Optimistic success that can become a lost phrase or wrong entitlement. |
| Device performance | Measure startup, frame behavior, memory and interaction latency against the unchanged build on representative iOS/Android devices. | A showcase that is smooth only in the browser or on a flagship phone. |
| Audio / privacy | Permission and recorder state remain the authority; all capture/audio stops as specified after exit or interruption. | Fake listening, fake lip-sync, unsolicited reward sound or an idle tutor loop. |

## F25: more colour through stronger useful moments

**Contract boundary:** palette.ts currently restricts lilac to flat art, chartreuse to one highlighter and forbids retired tint surfaces. The first proposal works within the current palette. Any later new tint/gradient/lilac surface requires an explicit token, contrast and design-contract revision; do not reintroduce forbidden values ad hoc.

### Current app is already colourful

O, S. Luvani has warm paper, white reading cards, deep ink and saturated blue, coral, sun and mint. Onboarding and the first scene already use bold fields. The issue is not absence of colour: some working screens have repeated neutral blocks while large art or a number receives more emphasis than the next useful task. More random colours would make that hierarchy less clear.

### Recommended visual direction

Keep the warm ground and Outfit typography. Make one primary next-task card or earned-result moment lead each working page. Bring the existing illustrated scene into that card at a modest size, with the task and French line first. Use the existing contrasting foregrounds; keep library lists, Settings and longer French passages on calm paper or white surfaces.

### Roles that retain meaning

Blue identifies the next scene or chosen path. Sun can lead a focus-sound card. Mint can emphasize a genuinely earned outcome or saved-state tick, with an explicit label distinguishing saved from mastered. Coral remains tied to tutor speaking and the established recap role. A save alone must not make a can-do level look earned. The voice orb keeps its current four-state map.

### Playful and premium

Use controlled saturation, stable spacing, expressive existing illustration and concise labels. One delightful event earns attention more effectively than colouring every row. Treat enjoyable voluntary return and useful French practice as outcomes to test. There is no defensible guarantee that a hex code makes the app addictive.

### First pass within current tokens

## Integrated implementation order and decision ownership

**Product/design owner:** prioritization, screen hierarchy, semantic colour and event eligibility. **Engineering owner:** accurate state wiring, save provenance, reduced-motion lifecycle and native runtime performance. **Learning reviewer:** French content and achievement scope. **Billing/privacy owners:** cost and processing statements. The report does not invent personnel or delivery dates.

**Measure:** finding the next action, completing a useful first attempt, understanding feedback and price, finding/reusing a kept phrase, voluntary useful return, perceived clutter and comfort. Compare source-matched builds and consented cohorts. Do not treat time spent watching animation, notification opens or a signup head start as French learning.

**Ship rule:** a visually more vibrant build still fails release if it creates simulated evidence, loses real work, hides costs or lacks the required native/deployed proofs. Once the documented blockers are resolved, validate copy/colour/motion changes with the existing acceptance kit and meaningful regression checks.

| Order | Concrete reviewable work | Required proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Trust | F01 isolation, accurate learning records and existing native/deployed release gates. | Production path, real recording/assessment scope and dated acceptance evidence. |
| 2. First value / copy | Task-first onboarding and briefing; F23 ledger; account-state-specific copy; remove duplicate explanation. | Small/large-text captures, beginner comprehension and preserved data/price facts. |
| 3. Colour hierarchy | One useful focal card per working screen; actual phrase prominence; source-token foregrounds. | Contrast, visible task before Start, colour-independent labels and meaning. |
| 4. Motion prototypes | M01 selection and M03 confirmed save first; M04 only eligible earned completion. | Reduced motion, interruption, failure, no duplicate work, physical-device performance. |
| 5. Optional expansion | Test navigation, reminders and richer one-shot art; evaluate a new runtime only for an established need. | Evidence of useful task/retrieval benefit; asset and platform budget; explicit design-contract revision. |
