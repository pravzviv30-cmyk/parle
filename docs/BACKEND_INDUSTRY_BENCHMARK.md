# Industry benchmark for the Luvani backend audit

Research date: 3 October 2026. This is a benchmark of **100 distinct app/service public disclosures**, not a ranked list of the world's 100 largest apps, an independent security certification, or an inspection of 100 complete private production architectures. The source register identifies technical articles, narrowly scoped security/API disclosures, operations disclosures, shared company evidence, and platform evidence separately. Historical articles demonstrate a pattern at publication time; they do not establish the app's complete current stack. Recommendations below are engineering inferences for Luvani and must be reconciled against the code and deployed configuration.

[OWASP API Security Top 10](https://api-security.owasp.org/editions/2023/en/0x11-t10/) | Treat database RLS, API ownership checks, mutable-field restrictions, and expensive-operation quotas as separate controls. ## Architectural guidance

For Luvani, a reasonable target is a managed Postgres/Auth/Storage control plane, a small typed API layer, a durable job mechanism, managed WebRTC media, bounded tutor workers, and centralized metrics/error tracking. Separate the **control plane** (identity, entitlement, room admission, session state, progress, usage), **media/agent runtime** (audio, turn handling, model and speech calls), and **post-session processing** (summary, assessment, notifications, reconciliation). This is a recommendation, not a statement that the current code already implements these boundaries.

The most valuable investment before launch is proving negative and failure cases: cross-user access denial, immutable premium/credit fields, replayed billing events, concurrent sessions, orphan workers, provider timeouts, reconnects, failed summaries, and restoring a usable account. Copying a famous app's technology without its operating discipline would not establish production readiness.

## Evidence limits and open questions

- Sources disclose a component, integration, or stated policy. A security-policy source has weaker assurance than a detailed engineering post and neither substitutes for an independent test.
- Shared company examples (such as King/Supercell or Expedia Group) are marked as shared. Their source does not establish a separately verified architecture for every branded app.
- Platform references (Open edX, GitLab architecture, Reddit Devvit) describe the platform; deployment-specific settings remain unknown.
- Historical examples include Duolingo lesson audio (2017), Khan Academy concurrency (2016), and Quizlet migration (2016). Their patterns are relevant; their current private stacks cannot be inferred.
- Public customer case studies can be selective and vendor-sponsored. Reported performance/cost outcomes are treated as the publisher's disclosure, not independent benchmark measurements.
- Neither this industry register nor a repository review proves deployed RLS, dashboard flags, branch rules, provider quotas, billing configuration, backup retention, or real tutor behavior unless those are inspected separately.

The completed register contains 100 distinct app/service entries and 100 distinct primary source URLs, with targeted disclosure verification for every entry: 94 used Firecrawl page content and 6 used primary-source content through the web reader after access failure or source correction. Scope counts: 61 app technical, 19 app security, 6 app API, 3 platform technical, 7 company technical, 2 subsystem technical, 1 app operations, and 1 company operations. No complete private production architecture was inspected. Source scope and review depth should accompany any claim that 100 apps were studied.
