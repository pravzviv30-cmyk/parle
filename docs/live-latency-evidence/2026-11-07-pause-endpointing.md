## Reading it

- "today" is minDelay 400 with Silero defaults (minSilenceDuration 550), so the turn commits at the VAD end of speech, not 400 ms after it.
- "eot" uses the real local model; "fallback-1.0" is what a worker without the native binding or a job inference executor does (every prediction scores 1.0).
- Every clip is the same sentence in six ElevenLabs voices, not learners. Hesitant, accented beginner speech is likely to score differently, so treat the model columns as indicative and the fallback columns as the floor that has to hold on its own.
- The `v009-*-max*` and `beginner-800-*` configs only change maxDelay, which applies when the turn model says "not finished" ("eot" detector); under "fallback-1.0" every prediction is 1.0 so maxDelay never applies and they match their minDelay twin.
- `dynamic-*` runs the SDK's own DynamicEndpointing object. It differs from the fixed rule only in where minDelay comes from: an EMA (alpha 0.9) of the speaker's between-utterance pauses, floored at minDelay and capped at maxDelay, updated at END_OF_SPEECH just before the wait is read, exactly as audio_recognition.js orders it. The commit rule itself (maxDelay when the model says "not finished", else the learned minDelay) is the same. `-t1` is a first turn (the pause in this turn moves the EMA by only 10 %, so it behaves like fixed); `-t11` is the eleventh turn of a speaker who paused this long in each of the ten before, an upper bound for a habitual pauser, since learners will not pause the same way every turn. The replay sends no agent-speech marks, which is what the SDK sees between a learner's own utterances.
- A finished sentence scored under the threshold in 1 of 36 clip runs (alexandre at 2500 ms, p 0.26). Those wait the whole maxDelay, which is the "max" figure in the eot table, so a higher maxDelay buys patience in a sentence and costs that much silence on the rare misread ending.
- Latency is measured from the end of the audio samples; Silero stops calling a frame voiced slightly before that, which is why today reads about 470 ms rather than 550.
